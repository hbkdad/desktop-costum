# Problem Ranking

Ranks the 4 candidate underserved segments from `docs/research/competitor-matrix.md` / `docs/research/market-gap-report.md` by: (a) how many of the 8 researched competitors leave it unresolved, (b) implementation risk against this project's non-negotiable principles, (c) monetization potential.

| Rank | Problem | Competitors leaving it unresolved | Implementation risk | Monetization potential | Net priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shell-update resilience | 8/8 (all have some breakage history; 5/8 tied to 24H2 specifically) | Medium — directly served by non-negotiables #2-5 (supported APIs, adapter isolation, overlay fallback, no undocumented-only dependency); risk is in execution discipline, not concept | Indirect (retention/trust driver, not a line item users pay for directly) — but retention is the precondition for all other monetization | **Highest** — foundational, low concept-risk, differentiates against literally every competitor |
| 2 | Resource-budget discipline | 3/3 rendering-heavy competitors (Wallpaper Engine, Lively, DeskScapes) — the products most likely to be this project's closest functional overlap | Low-Medium — mostly engineering discipline (budgets, adaptive rendering, CI performance gates), no novel technical risk | Direct — "works great on laptops" is a plausible premium/differentiating claim, and un-addressed battery drain is a documented uninstall driver | **High** — cheap to commit to, addresses the segment closest to core wallpaper/widget scope |
| 3 | Licensing clarity | 4/4 Stardock products show forum confusion (Fences, Start11, DeskScapes, Groupy); DisplayFusion shows separate price-friction complaints | Low — this is a pricing/communication decision already partly made (no subscription required for local features is a stated non-negotiable), not an engineering problem | Direct, but modest — clarity reduces churn/refund friction more than it drives net-new revenue | **Medium** — nearly free to claim given decisions already made; good goodwill, not a standalone growth lever |
| 4 | Consolidation (one product vs. Stardock's 4+ separate apps) | Structural gap unique to Stardock's product family (4/8 competitors), not the whole category | High — this is the entire scope of the platform (workspaces + containers + widgets + wallpapers + automation + creator tools), so "risk" here is really "this is the whole roadmap," not a single feature | High long-term, but unprovable pre-launch without shipping breadth | **Long-run structural, not MVP** — real advantage, but must be earned through Phase 5-7 execution, not claimed at MVP launch |

## Implication for MVP scope

Rank 1 and 2 should be explicit MVP acceptance criteria (see `docs/product/prd.md` once populated) with measurable checks (Explorer-restart recovery test, reported resource-impact numbers per `performance-test` skill). Rank 3 is satisfied by decisions already recorded in `.agents/state/decisions.md` and just needs to be communicated clearly at launch. Rank 4 is the multi-year thesis, not a Phase 5 deliverable — tracked but not promised early.
